# PART 1: Close-friends and Gen Z social apps

## 1. BeReal

- **Hook.** BeReal sends one notification, at the same unpredictable moment for everyone in a time zone. You get two minutes to take a front-and-back photo, and you can only see your friends' posts after you post your own. The pull is reciprocity: you can see others only by showing yourself. It also offers relief from curation, because there is no time to stage anything.
- **Virality.** Campus ambassadors, a paid referral program and the shared notification moment, when "it's BeReal time" became something people said out loud in a room. Launched December 2019 per TechCrunch, though Wikipedia gives 2020 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/04/22/bereal-hype-or-hit-what-to-know-about-the-gen-z-photo-sharing-app-climbing-the-charts/amp)) [V, launch year conflicts]. Topped the US App Store free chart from late July 2022 and was Apple's iPhone App of the Year for 2022 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BeReal)) [V, secondary].
- **Voice and design.** The help center calls the prompt the "⚠️Time to BeReal.⚠️ Notification" and says it is sent at a time BeReal does not disclose ([help.bereal.com](https://help.bereal.com/hc/articles/7350386715165)) [V]. Late posts are stamped with how late they were ([lx.com](https://lx.com/culture/social-media/bereal-users-have-figured-out-how-to-befake/57182)) [V]. The store listing is headed "BeReal. Your friends for real." and its description said: "BeReal won't make you famous, if you want to become an influencer you can stay on TikTok and Instagram" ([APKMirror listing copy](https://www.apkmirror.com/apk/bereal/bereal-your-friends-for-real); [McClatchy via Mahoning Matters](https://www.mahoningmatters.com/news/nation-world/article260178825.html)) [V, 2022-era listing copies; punctuation varies by source].
- **Why it decayed.** Usage peaked at roughly 70M DAU (company figure). Sensor Tower data cited by the NYT showed DAU down 61% from October 2022 to March 2023 ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/?p=180594)) [V]. Voodoo bought BeReal in June 2024 for about €500M, partly as earnouts, and later made layoffs ([PocketGamer.biz](https://pocketgamer.biz/bereal-hit-by-layoffs-following-voodoo-acquisition)) [V]. Voodoo claimed "40M+ active users" without saying daily, weekly or monthly ([Engadget](https://engadget.com/bereal-the-buzziest-app-of-2022-has-been-bought-by-a-mobile-game-publisher-175016152.html?src=rss)) [V]. Users "BeFaked" by waiting for a good moment. The daily ritual felt like a chore once the novelty wore off, and the "late" stamp turned authenticity into a compliance score.
- **Lesson for Switchboard.** A shared ritual moment is a powerful hook. But a ritual that shames you for missing it ("2 hours late") turns into an obligation. Standing rituals should never show lateness or absence to others. Switchboard's "give space" and private RSVP fit this. Copy their reciprocity only where it is mutual and consensual, never as a gate.

## 2. Locket Widget

- **Hook.** A friend's photo shows up on your home screen with no feed, no likes and no public profile. It feels like being present in someone's day, which is ambient intimacy.
- **Virality.** Matt Moss built it for his girlfriend and released it on January 1, 2022. A TikTok tutorial pushed it to number 1, with about 2M users in two weeks ([VnExpress](https://e.vnexpress.net/news/tech/personalities/how-a-gift-for-his-girlfriend-changes-locket-founder-matt-moss-s-life-5106478.html)) [V]. Every install needs a friend to install it too, so the product itself is the invite.
- **Voice and design.** The cap is 20 friends: the listing says "To keep things friendly, you can only have 20 friends on the app," and Apple's 2022 App Store Awards write-up describes dedicating the widget to a partner or "up to 20 of your closest friends" ([Apple](https://apps.apple.com/ca/story/id1647059807); [APKMirror listing copy](https://apkmirror.com/apk/locket-labs-inc/locket-widget/locket-widget-1-200-0-release)) [V]. The listing describes "a widget that shows you live pictures from your friends, right on your Home Screen" ([listing mirror](https://appstore-library.rotato.app/ios/app/com.locket.Locket)) [V-corrected: that is description text, not a confirmed subtitle].
- **Why it held.** As of August 2025: 80M+ downloads, 9M+ DAU, profitable in 2024 on about $12.5M raised, and a "Celebrity Lockets" feature for music artists ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/06/photo-sharing-app-locket-is-banking-on-a-new-celebrity-focused-feature-to-fuel-its-growth)) [V]. A later report says it is popular with Gen Alpha, with about 15 staff and 100k+ subscribers ([Substack](https://asseenonbyochuko.substack.com/p/gen-alphas-post-algorithm-social)) [V, single source]. It held because the unit is the pair or the small circle, and there is nothing to perform for.
- **Lesson.** A capped, tiny audience is a feature. Switchboard's surfaces (an upcoming plan, a "thinking of you" nudge) could live on the home screen or a PWA notification as ambient presence, not a feed. Keep groups small by default.

## 3. Retro

- **Hook.** A weekly photo journal that only your followers can see, with no public like counts or friend lists. Ex-Instagram founders Nathan Sharp and Ryan Olson (Lone Palm Labs, 2022) ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/07/07/retro-is-a-deeply-personal-photo-journaling-app-for-close-friends/); [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/companies/retro/)) [V].
- **Virality.** Slow and organic. Sharp pitches it as "the single best place to catch up with friends and family." Recaps, "Rewind" and physical postcards spread it ([TechCrunch Dec 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/12/07/retro-lets-you-create-recaps-of-your-most-memorable-photos-and-send-the-best-ones-as-postcards)) [V].
- **Voice and design.** A weekly cadence ("share at least one moment from your week"). Premium launched in 2025 at about $36 a year. About 7.8M downloads as of August 2026 per Dealroom, versus about 1M users per Fast Company ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91462432/retro-photo-sharing-app)) [V, figures conflict].
- **Why it held.** Weekly is a humane cadence. The product rewards catching up, not constant posting.
- **Lesson.** Weekly is the right default rhythm for Switchboard's reconnection radar and ritual check-ins. Memory capsules should borrow Retro's "recap" and "rewind" (a look back at plans that happened) rather than a feed.

## 4. tbh and Gas (Nikita Bier)

- **Hook.** Positive-only, anonymous polls about classmates ("Who has the best smile?"). The recipient learns that someone chose them, but not who. Being chosen anonymously gives a validation hit, with no cost to the person who chose.
- **Virality.** tbh went to number 1 in 2017. Facebook bought it on October 16, 2017 (price undisclosed; TechCrunch heard it was "less than $100 million") ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/10/16/facebook-acquires-anonymous-teen-compliment-app-tbh-will-let-it-run/)) [V-corrected] and shut it in July 2018 for "low usage" ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2018/07/02/facebook-shuts-down-tbh-hello-moves/)) [V]. Gas (August 2022) relaunched the idea. You sign up by school, and a school "unlocks" once enough students join. Bier's tactic of seeding hype per school is documented for tbh in a leaked 2018 Facebook memo: a private Instagram account per school followed students, then flipped public at the last school bell ([Boing Boing](https://boingboing.net/2018/08/08/manipulating-children.html)) [V]. Accounts of the same "velvet rope" playbook for Gas come from secondary posts ([Mostly Business](https://mostlybusiness.beehiiv.com/p/how-gas-went-viral-with-1-million)) [V, secondary]. Gas reached 7.4M installs and about $7M in spending. Discord bought it in January 2023 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_(app)); [LBC/Variety](https://www.lbcgroup.tv/news/variety/682893/discord-acquires-gas-a-compliments-based-social-me/en)) [V]. Discord announced the shutdown in October 2023 and closed Gas on November 7, 2023. Gas had reached 1M DAU within two months of launch but was downloaded only 2,900 times in September 2023 (data.ai), and Discord's CEO told staff the purchase was mainly for the engineering talent ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/19/discord-kills-gas-anonymous-compliments-app-bought-nine-months-ago)) [V].
- **Voice and design.** Senders are shown as vague labels like "a boy in 10th grade." "God Mode", a paid tier, gives hints about who sent it [V]. A false sex-trafficking hoax hit downloads and brought the team death threats [V].
- **Why it decayed.** Poll fatigue: once you've been "gassed" by everyone, the next one means less. It also earned money by selling hints that undermined the anonymity it promised, and it was tied to one school year.
- **Lesson.** Anonymous positive signal works, but it is a sugar high unless it leads somewhere real. Switchboard's mutual-interest matching should turn signal into a plan, not into more polls. **Never monetize de-anonymization.** God Mode is exactly the pattern Switchboard's RLS must make impossible. Bier's school-by-school density is the right launch model for a plans app: one friend group or campus at a time until it is dense enough to work.

## 5. Lapse

- **Hook.** A disposable camera: photos "develop" later, so you live in the moment and get a delayed reward. Motto "friends not followers" ([Ypulse](https://www.ypulse.com/newsfeed/2023/11/02/lapse-a-2021-launched-photo-app-with-the-motto-friends-not-followers-is-making-waves-in-the-social-media-scene/)) [V].
- **Virality.** In 2023 it required new users to invite (text) about five friends before using the app. That took it from number 118 to number 1. Critics called it a "pyramid scheme" ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2023/09/29/disposable-camera-app-lapse-tops-charts-by-making-users-invite-friends)) [V]. By November 2023, downloads were down about 70% from the October peak, and by March 2025 it was out of the US top 100 ([OnlineOptimism](https://www.onlineoptimism.com/blog/lapse-app-stats/)) [V]. $11M seed led by Octopus and GV [V].
- **Lesson.** Forced invites spike the charts and burn trust. Each forced text spends the inviter's social capital on the app's behalf. Switchboard's cascading invites must be the opposite: invites go out **because of a real plan**, one person at a time, and never as an activation toll.

## 6. Noplace

- **Hook.** A MySpace-style profile with text-only status updates ("what are you doing / listening to"), customizable colors, and friends and global feeds. Self-expression without photo pressure.
- **Virality.** A waitlist of about 500k, then number 1 on the App Store in July 2024 on leaving invite-only. $15M Series A1 at a $75M pre-money valuation ([Sherwood](https://sherwood.news/tech/noplace-launches-to-top-of-charts-but-can-the-social-media-app-sustain-the); [Business Insider NL](https://www.businessinsider.nl/noplace-is-the-hottest-new-app-on-the-charts-its-founder-spills-on-how-it-went-viral-and-plans-to-skirt-becoming-a-fad/)) [V]. It had dropped off the charts shortly after ([woshipm](https://www.woshipm.com/evaluating/6081902.html)) [V]. Status in 2025 and 2026: [U].
- **Lesson.** Nostalgic self-expression launches well but gives no reason to come back. There is no shared object (no plan, no event). A plans app has a built-in reason to return: the plan itself.

## 7. Airchat

- **Hook.** Voice posts, auto-transcribed and played as you scroll. Intimacy of the voice, plus a low barrier for people who don't like writing.
- **Virality.** Invite-only, seeded with tech personalities. Built by Naval Ravikant and Brian Norgard. About 50k downloads in April 2024 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/17/airchat-the-buzzy-new-social-app-could-be-great-or-it-could-succumb-to-the-same-fate-as-clubhouse); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/airchat-is-the-latest-app-trying-to-make-social-audio-cool-again-140050450.html?src=rss)) [V]. Current status [U].
- **Lesson.** A founder-network seed attracts an audience that performs for founders. Voice does build closeness (see Kumar and Epley in Part 2), so an optional short voice note on an invite ("come, it'll be fun") may beat text, but only inside an existing relationship.

## 8. Clubhouse

- **Hook.** Drop-in live audio: FOMO, plus the chance to be in the room with famous people.
- **Virality.** Invite-only (two invites each). Sharing your address book was the only way to invite, and the invite list showed how many friends each contact already had on Clubhouse ([Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/social-media/clubhouse-social-media-app-digital-privacy-security)); the app re-uploaded every number in the address book each time the invite tab was opened ([zerforschung](https://zerforschung.org/posts/clubhouse-telefonnummern-en)) [V; the exact sort order of the list is reported inconsistently]. Lockdown boredom. Valuation went from $1B in January 2021 to $4B a few months later ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/04/27/clubhouse-layoffs-app-reached-4-billion-valuation-during-pandemic.html)) [V].
- **Voice and design.** The "✌️ Leave quietly" button, which exits a room without notifying anyone, plus raise-hand and stage metaphors ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clubhouse_(app))) [V]. The founders later said it had become "harder for many people to find their friends on Clubhouse and to fit long conversations into their daily lives" as the world reopened. About half the staff were laid off in April 2023, and it pivoted to small-group voice "Chats" ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2023/04/28/clubhouse-layoffs-cuts-more-than-half-its-staff)) [V].
- **Lesson.** Two lessons. (1) "Leave quietly" is a great anti-awkwardness primitive, and Switchboard's "give space" and quiet RSVP-decline are in the same family. (2) Live, synchronous, open-ended time is expensive. People retreated to their friends. A plans app should help schedule **bounded** time with known people.

## 9. Yik Yak and Fizz

- **Yik Yak.** Anonymous posts within about a 5-mile radius, plus up and down votes. Geofences were added around high schools after bullying ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Yik_Yak)) [V, single source]. Downloads fell 76% in 2016, and it shut on May 5, 2017. Block bought the IP for $1M, relaunched it in August 2021 with "community guardrails", and Sidechat acquired it around 2023 ([Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/2022/03/07/yik-yak-re-emerges-after-shutdown); [Failory](https://www.failory.com/cemetery/yik-yak)) [V].
- **Fizz.** Stanford, 2021. Posting requires a .edu email, and each campus has student moderators. "Controlled launches" campus by campus, with paper slips under doors and payments to clubs per signup. Hundreds of campuses, ad-funded ([Stanford Daily](https://stanforddaily.com/2022/10/05/stanford-startup-fizz-secures-4-5-million-in-seed-funding-targets-nationwide-expansion/); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/405653/the-fizz-ad-biz-a-conversation-with-teddy-solomon.html)) [V].
- **Lesson.** Bounded communities (a campus, a radius) create density and relevance. But anonymity aimed **at** named people turns into harassment. Switchboard's anonymity runs the other way: it protects the **voter or the person interested**, never a speaker who is saying something about someone. Keep it that way. Anonymous aggregate signals only, never anonymous free text about a person.

## 10. Poparazzi

- **Hook.** "Your friends are your paparazzi." You cannot post photos of yourself; only friends can. Being hyped by others is a form of validation by proxy.
- **Virality.** About 1.1M US and 2M global downloads in its first week of May 2021 ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/media-entertainment/what-you-need-to-know-about-poparazzi-the-new-photo-sharing-app-that-doesnt-allow-selfies/)) [V]. Shut down in 2023 despite 5M+ installs and a $15M Series A ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/05/01/once-hot-photo-sharing-social-app-poparazzi-is-shutting-down)) [V].
- **Lesson.** A clever constraint is a launch, not a habit. It still needed a feed to retain people, and the feed was performative again, now by proxy. Room photos in Switchboard should belong to the **plan's participants**, made by and for the people who were there, with no outward audience.

## 11. Dispo

- **Hook.** A disposable camera where photos "develop" at 9 a.m. local time the next morning ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2021/02/16/retro-photo-app-is-winning-buzz)) [V]. "Live in the moment."
- **Virality.** David Dobrik's audience. The March 2021 allegations against his Vlog Squad led Dobrik to step away and Spark Capital to cut ties ([Dead Pixels Society](https://thedeadpixelssociety.com/david-dobrik-steps-away-from-dispo-camera-app/)) [V]. Current status [U].
- **Lesson.** Influencer-led growth borrows someone else's reputation and its risk. Delayed reveal is a strong memory mechanic. Switchboard's memory capsule could "develop" the morning after a plan, shared only with attendees.

## 12. Geneva

- **Hook.** "The home for your groups": club and community chat for interest groups.
- **Outcome.** Bumble bought it in May 2024. It generated zero revenue as of June 2025, and it was shut and folded into the September 2025 Bumble BFF relaunch, with groups migrated ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/18/bumble-bffs-revamped-app-is-here-focusing-on-friend-groups-and-community-building)) [V].
- **Lesson.** Group chat is a commodity owned by iMessage, WhatsApp and Discord. Winning the "group" means winning **the thing groups do** (meet), not another chat surface. Switchboard should not build a chat app, and should hand off to existing chats where possible.

## 13. Marco Polo

- **Hook.** An asynchronous "video walkie-talkie" with no scheduling. You see a face and hear a voice on your own time.
- **Outcome.** 10M+ downloads by 2020. Marco Polo Plus launched in April 2020 at $59.99 a year, with backlash over features moving behind the paywall ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/04/29/video-app-marco-polo-adds-a-subscription-option-amid-coronavirus-led-boost-in-usage); [PhoneArena](https://www.phonearena.com/news/users-upset-after-video-messaging-app-marco-polo-disseminates-premium-version_id124318)) [V].
- **Why it held.** Families and long-distance friends. It is a utility for keeping ties warm, not a stage.
- **Lesson.** Async presence maintains outer-layer ties cheaply (Dunbar's decay, Part 2). It is a good model for reconnection radar: low-pressure "thinking of you" signals that need no reply.

## 14. Instagram's "close friends" turn

- **What happened.** Close Friends for Stories launched globally on November 30, 2018, after about 17 months of testing ([Instagram](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/curate-instagram-stories-for-close-friends-only)) [V], then expanded to feed posts and Reels in 2023 ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-rolls-out-option-share-reels-feed-posts-with-close-friends/699780/)) [V]. Adam Mosseri has said teens spend more time in DMs than Stories, and more in Stories than feed. Instagram moved resources, at one point the entire Stories team, to messaging ([Doug Belshaw summary](https://archive.dougbelshaw.com/thought-shrapnel/its-all-about/)) [V, secondary]. "Finstas", private second accounts for real friends, came first, and so did "photo dumps" as anti-curation [R].
- **Lesson.** Even the biggest performance platform admits that intimate sharing moved to small audiences and DMs. Mosseri said Close Friends could encourage more frequent sharing because only trusted people see it [V]. Smaller audiences increase sharing. Switchboard's default audience for any plan should be the smallest one that makes the plan happen. Cascading invites put this into practice.

## Cross-cutting synthesis for Part 1

1. **Intimacy scales by shrinking audiences, not growing them.** Locket's 20, Retro's followers, Close Friends, finstas.
2. **Constraint launches, utility retains.** BeReal's two minutes, Poparazzi's no-selfies and Lapse's develop delay all spiked, then faded. Locket and Marco Polo, which do a job, held.
3. **Density before breadth.** tbh, Gas and Fizz launched one school at a time. Clubhouse and Noplace launched to everyone and found no friends there.
4. **Forced virality spends the user's social capital.** Lapse's five-text gate and Clubhouse's contact-book ranking both did this. Invites should be a side effect of real plans.
5. **Anonymity protects receivers or harms targets, depending on direction.** tbh and Gas sold de-anonymization (God Mode). Yik Yak and Fizz enabled pile-ons. Switchboard's anonymity must protect the vulnerable party and never be for sale.
6. **Ritual becomes obligation when absence is visible.** BeReal's late stamp shows this.
7. **Novelty decays in about 6 to 12 months** for apps with no external object. Clubhouse lost its point when lockdown ended, and BeReal fell 61% in five months. A plans app's object is the real-world meeting, which renews itself.

# PART 2: The science and context of social connection

## A. The public-health framing

**A1. US Surgeon General advisory, "Our Epidemic of Loneliness and Isolation" (May 2023).** Vivek Murthy framed social disconnection as a public-health threat, with risks comparable to well-known ones. Loneliness is a subjective state, and isolation is the objective number and quality of contacts. The advisory proposes a national strategy built on six pillars ([HHS PDF](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/surgeon-general-social-connection-advisory.pdf); [USU summary](https://extension.usu.edu/news/highlights-from-the-us-surgeon-generals-advisory-on-loneliness.php)) [V for existence and framing]. The six pillars are: strengthen social infrastructure in local communities, enact pro-connection public policies, mobilize the health sector, reform digital environments, deepen our knowledge, and cultivate a culture of connection ([CNN via KTVZ](https://ktvz.com/health/cnn-health/2023/05/02/surgeon-general-lays-out-framework-to-tackle-loneliness-and-mend-the-social-fabric-of-our-nation/); [SPRC](https://sprc.org/?p=24184)) [V; press paraphrases, exact heading wording not seen in the PDF]. The advisory says the mortality impact of disconnection is similar to "smoking up to 15 cigarettes a day" ([AP via ABC17](https://abc17news.com/news/ap-national-news/2023/05/02/loneliness-poses-risks-as-deadly-as-smoking-surgeon-general/)) [V; one AP version said "a dozen"]. That comparison traces to Holt-Lunstad, Smith and Layton (2010, *PLoS Medicine*, [doi:10.1371/journal.pmed.1000316](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1000316)): 148 studies, 308,849 participants, a weighted odds ratio of 1.50 (95% CI 1.42 to 1.59) for survival with stronger social relationships ([DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/c9833ba608474b048444b92ae7ee48ad)) [V]. Quote it as "50% higher odds of survival", not "50% more likely to survive". The cigarette figure is a later comparison, not a claim in the 2010 paper itself. Time use: Americans overall spent about 20 minutes a day in person with friends in 2020, down from about 60 minutes in 2003, and 15 to 24 year olds saw about a 70% drop ([AP via ABC17](https://abc17news.com/news/ap-national-news/2023/05/02/loneliness-poses-risks-as-deadly-as-smoking-surgeon-general/); [NewsNation](https://digital-release.newsnationnow.com/?p=2262919)) [V]. The youth minutes-per-day figures (often quoted as 150 down to 40) [R] (unconfirmed after recheck). Its digital-environment pillar asks technology companies to be transparent with data and to design for healthy connection rather than engagement [R] (unconfirmed after recheck). **Implication:** Switchboard can position itself as a "reform digital environments" product: its north star is in-person time, not screen time. The advisory's individual recommendations (reach out, join groups, serve others, put the phone away during conversations) [R] (unconfirmed after recheck; press confirms a section for individuals but not its contents) map directly onto features: reconnection radar is "reach out", standing rituals are "join groups", and a quiet in-plan mode is "put the phone away".

**A2. Murthy's "Parting Prescription for America" (January 7, 2025).** A closing report that frames community, relationships, service and purpose as the base of well-being. It blames the erosion of community partly on technology and the loss of face-to-face conversation ([Psychiatrist.com](https://psychiatrist.com/news/surgeon-general-drafts-one-last-prescription); [PDF](https://www.vivekmurthy.com/_files/ugd/9322fa_6d33185c1dab47ffa16f238f35b3b537.pdf)) [V]. I found no 2025 or 2026 federal follow-up advisory on loneliness [V for absence in search results; not exhaustive]. **Implication:** the policy tailwind is real but no longer driven by federal action. Switchboard's credibility will come from its own outcome data (plans that happened), not borrowed authority.

**A3. WHO Commission on Social Connection, "From loneliness to social connection" (June 30, 2025).** About 1 in 6 people worldwide are lonely, linked to about 871,000 deaths a year (about 100 an hour). Among 13 to 29 year olds, 17% to 21% report loneliness, highest among teenagers. Isolation affects up to 1 in 3 older adults and 1 in 4 adolescents ([WHO news release](https://www.who.int/NEWS/ITEM/30-06-2025-SOCIAL-CONNECTION-LINKED-TO-IMPROVED-HEATH-AND-REDUCED-RISK-OF-EARLY-DEATH); [WHO Commission](https://www.who.int/groups/commission-on-social-connection)) [V]. The report links loneliness to cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, depression and anxiety [V]. **Implication:** young users are the loneliest cohort worldwide, which supports a mobile-first, youth-friendly product. But "loneliness" should never be used as marketing pressure ("you're lonely, open the app"). Copy should name the outcome (seeing people), not the deficit.

## B. The friendship recession

**B1. Survey Center on American Life, "The State of American Friendship" (May 2021 survey).** The share of Americans with no close friends rose from 3% in 1990 to 12% in 2021. For men it went from 3% to 15%. Having a best friend fell from 75% to 59%, and having ten or more close friends fell from 33% to 13% ([summary via WEF](https://weforum.org/agenda/2022/11/friendships-less-is-now-more); [National Review](https://www.nationalreview.com/2021/07/american-men-suffer-a-friendship-recession/amp/); primary: americansurveycenter.org, not reachable this session) [V via secondary sources]. Caveats: the 1990 baseline is a Gallup poll, and 2021 was mid-pandemic. **Implication:** many users will have small or eroded networks. Product features should work with 2 to 5 people. A plan with two people is a full success state, not an empty state.

**B2. American Time Use Survey (BLS).** Average daily time socializing fell from about 45 to 35 minutes over 20 years ([Axios, July 5, 2026](https://www.axios.com/2026/07/05/americans-socializing-decline)) [V]. One analysis reports 15 to 24 year olds fell from 61 minutes a day in 2003 to 35 in 2025, a 43% drop ([Hedgeye](https://app.hedgeye.com/insights/184658-howe-america-s-social-life-is-shrinking/print)) [V, secondary]. A counter-analysis of the raw time-with-friends variable finds adult time with friends "remarkably stable", with the dramatic drop concentrated in **teenagers** ([Omnibudsman](https://omnibudsman.substack.com/p/do-americans-even-make-friends-in)) [V]. Measures differ (socializing versus time with friends), so cite carefully. Related: Derek Thompson's "The Anti-Social Century", the February 2025 cover story of The Atlantic (online January 2025), synthesizes ATUS data on falling in-person socializing (about 20% less than 20 years earlier, in his words) and record time alone ([The Atlantic](https://www.theatlantic.com/magazine/archive/2025/02/american-loneliness-personality-politics/681091/); [Big Think interview](https://bigthink.com/series/the-big-think-interview/anti-social-century/)) [V]. **Implication:** the decline is real but measured inconsistently. Switchboard should track its own direct measure (hours together at plans), not proxies.

**B3. Young men.** Gallup (May 2025, 2023 to 2024 data): 25% of US men aged 15 to 34 felt lonely "a lot of the previous day," against 18% of young women, about 17% of other adults, and about 15% of young men across high-income OECD countries. Only Turkey was higher, at 29% ([UPI](https://www.upi.com/Health_News/2025/05/20/young-men-loneliness/4341747781728/); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/local/san-francisco/2025/05/30/loneliness-men-friends-gallup)) [V]. **Implication:** men tend to bond "side by side" around activities [R, general finding]. Activity-first plan templates (a run, a game, a match, building something) and low-disclosure invites ("I'm going to X, want in?") may serve young men better than "let's catch up."

## C. How friendships form

**C1. Hall's hours-to-friendship (2018).** About 50 hours of time together to go from acquaintance to casual friend, about 90 to reach friend, and more than 200 to reach close friend. Leisure time (hanging out, joking, games) counts; time working together counts much less. Based on 355 adults who had recently moved (Study 1, online panel), plus 112 college freshmen followed over time (Study 2). The press release gives point estimates (50, 90, 200+); secondary coverage gives ranges (40 to 60, 80 to 100, 200+). The thresholds are where the probability of moving up a stage rises by about 50%, so they are not guarantees ([KU News](https://news.ku.edu/2018/03/06/study-reveals-number-hours-it-takes-make-friend); Hall, "How many hours does it take to make a friend?", *J. Social and Personal Relationships*, [doi:10.1177/0265407518761225](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0265407518761225)) [V]. **Implication:** friendship is built from cumulative hours. Switchboard could privately show each user "hours together this season" with a person, as a gentle compass and never a public score or streak. Standing rituals (one 2-hour dinner a month adds up to 24 hours a year) are the fastest legitimate route to the 50 and 90 hour marks.

**C2. Rebecca Adams' three conditions.** Since the 1950s, sociologists have identified three conditions for friendship: proximity, repeated unplanned interaction, and a setting that lets people let their guard down and confide. In adulthood these come apart, which is why college is so friend-dense ([NYT 2012, "Friends of a Certain Age"](https://www.nytimes.com/2012/07/15/fashion/the-challenge-of-making-friends-as-an-adult.html); [UNCG](https://hhs.uncg.edu/?p=11185)) [V]. **Implication:** this is the core design brief. An app cannot create **unplanned** interaction directly, but it can make repetition nearly free. Standing rituals ("every second Thursday at the same place, come if you can") create **predictable** repetition, which is close to unplanned in practice: you don't have to decide each time. Low-stakes, small-group, recurring formats create the safety to confide.

**C3. Mere-exposure and proximity.** Zajonc (1968), "Attitudinal effects of mere exposure", *JPSP Monograph Supplement* 9(2, Pt. 2), 1 to 27, showed with nonsense words, Chinese-like characters and yearbook photos that repeated exposure increases liking ([doi:10.1037/h0025848](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0025848); [Scinapse](https://www.scinapse.io/papers/2096648510)) [V]. Festinger, Schachter and Back (1950, *Social Pressures in Informal Groups*) found friendships in MIT's Westgate housing, where units were assigned rather than chosen, followed physical distance and "functional distance" (the paths people walked past, such as stairwells) ([Wikipedia: Propinquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propinquity)) [V, secondary]. Moreland and Beach (1992, *J. Experimental Social Psychology* 28, 255 to 276) planted four similar-looking women in a large lecture course, attending 0, 5, 10 or 15 sessions without interacting with anyone; at semester's end students rated the more frequent attenders as more familiar, attractive and similar ([UMich Deep Blue listing](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/handle/2027.42/23882?show=full); [study notes](https://rune-sage-64b.notion.site/Moreland-and-Beach-Mere-Exposure-35d423a203d38202826301c5c36d8d49)) [V for citation and design; direction of results from standard textbook accounts, not seen in a primary source this pass]. **Implication:** simply showing up repeatedly, even quietly, builds liking. Rituals should make low-participation attendance ("I'll come but I'm low battery") welcome and visible as an option. The social-battery setting fits this.

**C4. Dunbar's layers.** Personal networks nest in layers of about 5, 15, 50 and 150, each roughly three times the one inside it ([Dunbar's numbers summary](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/holocene/dunbar.htm)) [V, secondary]. Roberts and Dunbar (2011), "The costs of family and friends: an 18-month longitudinal study of relationship maintenance and decay", *Evolution and Human Behavior* 32(3), 186 to 197 ([doi:10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2010.08.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2010.08.005); [Oxford ORA](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:0a8fe8a0-ee1a-47de-b70f-06da958af4a5)), followed 25 students moving from school to university and found friendships, unlike kin ties, lost emotional closeness when contact and shared activities fell [V-corrected: the DOI previously given here, 10.1111/j.1475-6811.2010.01310.x, belongs to a different 2011 Roberts and Dunbar paper in *Personal Relationships*; small sample, one life transition]. A 2015 follow-up (*Human Nature*) found more frequent contact slowed decay most for women, and doing things together most for men ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12110-015-9242-7)) [V]. Dunbar has said social media may "slow down the rate of decay" but cannot prevent it "if they are not occasionally reinforced by face-to-face interaction" ([Big Think](https://bigthink.com/surprising-science/facebook-isnt-about-connecting-friends-its-about-amassing-a-network-of-diverse-ideas/)) [V for quote]. **Implication:** reconnection radar should be layer-aware. The inner 5 need regular IRL time (rituals). The 15 and 50 need periodic re-warming (a radar nudge every few months). Do not encourage people to manage 150 relationships in the app. The "monthly contact keeps someone in the 15" rule circulating online has no traceable source [U].

**C5. Weak ties.** Granovetter (1973, *American Journal of Sociology*) argued weak ties carry new information and opportunity. In his sample of professional, technical and managerial workers in Newton, Massachusetts, among the 54 who found their job through a personal contact, 16.7% saw that contact often, 55.6% occasionally and 27.8% rarely, so about 83% used a contact they saw only occasionally or rarely ([Wikipedia: Interpersonal ties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpersonal_ties); [AcaWiki](https://acawiki.org/The_strength_of_weak_ties); [doi:10.1086/225469](https://doi.org/10.1086/225469)) [V-corrected: the earlier wording "most job-finders" overstated it; the figures describe contact-based hires only, and the DOI is from memory]. Sandstrom and Dunn (2014) found that on days people had more weak-tie interactions (classmates, the barista), they reported more happiness and belonging, controlling for extraversion and strong ties ([World Database of Happiness](https://worlddatabaseofhappiness.eur.nl/publications/social-interactions-and-well-being-the-surprising-power-of-weak-ties-12167/); [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/the-big-questions/201809/the-importance-of-weak-ties)) [V; causality unclear]. **Implication:** cascading invites (friend of a friend, one hop) create weak ties at low risk. "+1 a friend-of-a-friend" options and open-to-the-ritual sign-ups are good. Unbounded stranger discovery is not.

## D. The asking problem: why nobody makes the plan

**D1. People underestimate how much others appreciate being reached out to (Liu, Rim, Min and Min, JPSP, online July 2022).** Across experiments with more than 5,900 participants, initiators consistently underestimated how much recipients appreciated a check-in. The gap is largest when contact is **unexpected**, for example from weak ties. Recipients focus on the surprise ([APA release](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2022/07/friends-enjoy-being-reached); [doi:10.1037/pspi0000402](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspi0000402)) [V]. **Implication:** reconnection radar should cite this in its nudge: "People tend to appreciate hearing from someone more than the sender expects, especially out of the blue." Make the first touch tiny (one tap "thinking of you", with an optional plan attached).

**D2. The liking gap (Boothby, Cooney, Sandstrom and Clark, *Psychological Science*, 2018).** After conversations, people underestimate how much their partner liked them. This held for strangers in the lab, dorm mates over months, and workshop attendees. Liking is signaled but missed, because people focus on their own performance ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liking_gap); [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/blog/illusion-chasers/people-like-you-more-than-you-know); [doi:10.1177/0956797618783714](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797618783714)) [V]. **Implication:** this is the strongest case for the post-plan prompt. After a plan, ask each attendee privately, "Would you want to do this again with them?" When **both** say yes (mutual consent, so a target never learns of unrequited interest), reveal: "They'd like to see you again too." This closes the liking gap without exposing anyone. It is the same mechanism as mutual-interest matching, applied after the fact.

**D3. Overestimating rejection when asking (Flynn and Lake [Bohns], "If you need help, just ask: Underestimating compliance with direct requests for help", JPSP 95(1), 128 to 143, 2008).** Across experimental and field settings, requesters underestimated by as much as 50% how likely others were to agree to a direct request for help. Study 6 traced it to help-seekers neglecting the social cost of refusing ([Cornell eCommons full text](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/entities/publication/9c0adbfb-985c-4f7d-aae3-96959822bc4c); [Stanford GSB](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/news/research/flynn_ask.html); [doi:10.1037/0022-3514.95.1.128](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.95.1.128)) [V]. The "average underestimate of about 48%" figure (unconfirmed after recheck; use "as much as 50%"). **Two implications that pull in different directions.** (1) Asking works better than people think, so nudge people to ask. (2) Some of those yeses are **awkward yeses**, which later turn into flakes. Switchboard's private, anonymous RSVP and polls remove the awkwardness of saying no. The yeses you get are then real, which should lower flake rates. That is the honest pitch for private RSVPs.

**D4. Declining hurts less than people think (Givi and Kirk, "Saying no: The negative ramifications from invitation declines are less severe than we think", JPSP, published online December 11, 2023; often cited as 2024).** Five experiments with more than 2,000 participants: invitees expected inviters to feel angry, disappointed and less willing to invite them again, but inviters' actual reactions were milder and they focused on the reason given. In a pilot, 77% said they had accepted an invitation to something they did not want to attend for fear of the consequences of declining. Givi cautions that repeated declines can still reduce future invitations ([APA press release](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2023/12/say-no-invitation); [PsyPost](https://psypost.org/new-psychology-study-reveals-we-overestimate-the-consequences-of-declining-social-invitations); doi:10.1037/pspi0000443) [V-corrected: year is online 2023]. **Implication:** pair "can't make it" with a warm default ("Can't this time, ask me again") so declining does not end the thread. Never show who declined to the group, only to the host if they opt in.

**D5. Rejection sensitivity (Downey and Feldman, JPSP 1996).** Some people anxiously expect rejection, read it into ambiguous cues and react strongly, which harms close relationships ([PDF](https://www.ffri.hr/~ibrdar/komunikacija/seminari/Downey,%201996%20-%20Rejection%20Sensitivity%20for%20intimate%20relationsh.pdf)) [V]. **Implication:** silence is the cue most likely to be misread. Never show "seen, no reply." A non-response should resolve to a neutral state ("Not this time") after a deadline, not hang open. Cascading invites help: the host never sees a broadcast with five silences, only the next confirmed yes.

**D6. The spotlight effect (Gilovich, Medvec and Savitsky, JPSP 2000).** People overestimate how much others notice them. Students wearing an embarrassing Barry Manilow shirt guessed about half the room noticed (one source gives a mean guess of 46%), while actually fewer than a quarter did (about 23%) ([Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/between-you-and-me/201311/have-you-fallen-prey-to-the-spotlight-effect); [Cornell Alumni Magazine](https://cornellalumnimagazine.com/things-that-make-you-go-hmm); [doi:10.1037/0022-3514.78.2.211](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.78.2.211)) [V for study and direction; the 46% and 23% figures are from secondary sources, so say "about half" and "under a quarter"]. **Implication:** a host's failed plan feels public to them. Hide failure: when a cascade ends with nobody, tell only the host, kindly ("Nobody could make Thursday. That's timing, not you. Try another day?"). Never notify invitees that a plan collapsed if they hadn't joined.

**D7. Pluralistic ignorance: everyone wants to hang out, nobody asks.** Group members systematically misjudge peers' private attitudes. The classic case is Prentice and Miller (1993) on campus drinking norms ([review, Frontiers 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/social-psychology/articles/10.3389/frsps.2023.1260896/pdf)) [V]. A related finding: people underestimate how often others feel negative emotions (partly because negative feelings are more often hidden), and lower estimates go with more loneliness and rumination and lower life satisfaction. The link is correlational ("Misery has more company than people think: Underestimating the prevalence of others' negative emotions," Jordan, Monin, Dweck, Lovett, John and Gross, *PSPB* 37(1), 2011; [BPS Research Digest](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/other-people-may-experience-more-misery-you-realise); [TIME](https://healthland.time.com/?p=23748)) [V for lead authors, journal, year and findings; the full author list and volume are from memory]. A GWU dissertation links "emotional pluralistic ignorance" to loneliness through friendship dissatisfaction ([GWU](https://scholarspace.library.gwu.edu/concern/gw_etds/v979v3770)) [V, unreviewed thesis]. I found no peer-reviewed study that tests "everyone wants to hang out but nobody asks" directly [U], so present it as an inference from D1 to D3. **Implication:** mutual-interest matching is the cure for pluralistic ignorance. It reveals shared desire only when it is shared. An aggregate, anonymous "3 people in this circle are up for something this weekend" signal (with a minimum group size, never naming anyone) can unlock the first ask.

**D8. Anticipated versus actual awkwardness (Epley and Schroeder, *JEP: General*, 2014).** Chicago commuters predicted that solitude would be nicer than talking to a stranger, but those told to talk had more pleasant rides, and nobody reported being snubbed. Commuters assumed fewer than half of strangers would want to talk ([Chicago Booth](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/talk-to-a-stranger-itll-make-you-happier); [BPS Research Digest](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/were-happier-when-we-chat-strangers-our-instinct-ignore-them)) [V]. Related: Kumar and Epley (2021), "It's surprisingly nice to hear you", *JEP: General* 150(3), 595 to 607 (doi:10.1037/xge0000962). People reconnecting with an old friend by phone or email, and strangers chatting by video, voice or text, expected voice to be more awkward, but voice produced stronger bonds with no measurable difference in awkwardness ([Nautilus](https://nautil.us/people-are-discovering-the-joy-of-actually-talking-on-a-phone-237971); [Uni Mannheim](https://www.uni-mannheim.de/forschung-erleben/artikel/schoen-deine-stimme-zu-hoeren/)) [V]. **Implication:** a plan's pre-event friction is mostly anticipation. Switchboard can say so at the point of hesitation ("Most people find these go better than they expected"). It can also offer an optional voice note on invites and a "5 minute call to pick a time" instead of a long thread.

**D9. The beautiful mess effect (Bruk, Scholl and Bless, JPSP 2018).** People judge their own vulnerability (admitting a mistake, saying "I miss you") as weakness, but judge others' identical displays as positive and courageous. Across six or seven studies, the gap is explained by construal level ([Mannheim](https://madoc.bib.uni-mannheim.de/48455); [BPS](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/beautiful-mess-effect); [doi:10.1037/pspa0000120](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspa0000120)) [V]. **Implication:** "I miss hanging out" feels riskier to send than it is to receive. Offer short, vulnerable prompt templates ("It's been too long, I miss you. Coffee?") and say plainly that recipients usually read them as warm. Mutual-consent reveals reduce the risk further.

## E. Making plans happen

**E1. Choice overload and decision friction.** Iyengar and Lepper (2000, "When choice is demotivating", JPSP) ran a grocery tasting booth with 24 or 6 jams: about 3% of those who stopped at the 24-jam display bought, versus about 30% at the 6-jam display ([AcaWiki](https://acawiki.org/When_Choice_is_Demotivating:_Can_One_Desire_Too_Much_of_a_Good_Thing%3F); [doi:10.1037/0022-3514.79.6.995](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.79.6.995)) [V]. But Scheibehenne, Greifeneder and Todd (2010, *J. Consumer Research* 37(3), 409 to 425) pooled 63 conditions from 50 experiments (N = 5,036) and found "a mean effect size of virtually zero but considerable variance between studies", with possible preconditions but no sufficient conditions for overload ([PDF](https://abcwest.cogs.indiana.edu/pmwiki/pdf/scheibehenne.options.2010.pdf); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/v37y2010i3p409-425.html)) [V]. "Decision fatigue" and ego depletion have also had replication failures [R]. **Implication:** don't build on "decision fatigue" as settled science. The robust design point is narrower: **fewer, concrete options with a default** convert better. Polls should offer 2 to 3 time options and a proposed place, not an open "when works for everyone?"

**E2. The group-chat death spiral (cultural observation, not a study).** The pattern: "We should hang out!" gets ten hearts, nobody proposes a time, the thread scrolls away, and the plan dies. Nobody owns the next step, which is a diffusion of responsibility in the bystander-effect sense (Darley and Latané 1968 [R]). I found no peer-reviewed study of group-chat planning specifically [U]. **Implication:** every Switchboard plan has exactly one owner and one next action. Enthusiasm without a time ("we should...") should be turned into a concrete proposal with one tap. Close the poll on a deadline and auto-pick the winner.

**E3. Flaking norms.** Cancelling is socially easier in text, and as D3 suggests, some yeses were never real. Givi and Kirk (D4) suggest people overrate the social cost of saying no up front [V]. **Implication:** make "maybe" honest and "no" cheap before the plan, so that "yes" is a real commitment afterward. A gentle day-of "still on?" check, plus a cascading backfill from the waitlist when someone drops, protects the host. Don't publicly track flake rates; reliability scores are shame mechanics (see BeReal's late stamp).

**E4. Rituals.** A review of ritual psychology (Hobson, Schroeder, Risen, Xygalatas and Inzlicht, "The psychology of rituals: An integrative review and process-based framework", *Personality and Social Psychology Review* 22(3), 260 onward, 2018) proposes that rituals regulate emotion, performance states and social connection ([Inzlicht lab PDF](https://michaelinzlicht.com/publications/articles-chapters/2017/9/7/the-psychology-of-rituals-an-integrative-review-and-process-based-framework-pdf); [doi:10.1177/1088868317734944](https://doi.org/10.1177/1088868317734944)) [V for citation; summary of content from memory, abstract not read this pass]. Norton and Gino (2014, *JEP: General* 143(1), 266 to 272, doi:10.1037/a0031772) found across three experiments that rituals reduced grief after losses of loved ones, lovers and lotteries, mediated by regained feelings of control, for believers and skeptics alike ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23398180/)) [V]. **Implication:** standing rituals ("first Sunday brunch") remove the asking problem entirely: once agreed, nobody has to initiate again. Rituals are also Adams' "repeated" condition in product form. Let people attend at whatever level of participation they can, without penalty.

**E5. Phones during time together.** Dwyer, Kushlev and Dunn (2018), "Smartphone use undermines enjoyment of face-to-face social interactions", *J. Experimental Social Psychology* 78, 233 to 239 (doi:10.1016/j.jesp.2017.10.007). In a field experiment, more than 300 people ate a restaurant meal with friends or family with phones on the table or put away; phones increased distraction, which lowered enjoyment. An experience-sampling study (about 123 people, five surveys a day for a week) found the same pattern ([PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/study-finds-smartphone-use-undermines-enjoyment-face-face-social-interactions/); [UBC thesis](https://open.library.ubc.ca/media/stream/pdf/24/1.0354413/4)) [V; effects are modest]. **Implication:** the best Switchboard session during a plan is no session. Go quiet during the plan window (no notifications), and bring up memory capsules and room photos the morning after.

## F. Third places, run clubs and supper clubs

**F1. Third places (Oldenburg, *The Great Good Place*, 1989).** Third places are neutral, accessible spaces with regulars and conversation as the main activity (cafés, pubs, barbershops). They are where Adams' unplanned interaction happens. Commentators blame their decline on car-oriented zoning, rising costs, remote work and home entertainment [R]. I could not verify a quantitative decline series this session [U]. **Implication:** Switchboard can act as a **portable third place**: a recurring "same place, same time, drop in if you can" ritual recreates the regulars dynamic without a venue owning it. Suggest consistent venues over novel ones; a familiar place is part of the ritual.

**F2. Run clubs.** Strava reported running-club participation up 59% worldwide in its 2024 trends report, and new clubs about tripled in 2025 ([Strides 'n' Miles](https://stridesnmiles.beehiiv.com/p/2025-in-review-strava-and-garmin-s-annual-report-deep-dive-7-world-marathon-majors-by-numbers-top-5); [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/strava-mid-year-data-running-cycling)) [V, secondary write-ups of Strava]. More than half of Gen Z respondents cited social connection as their main reason for joining a fitness group ([GearJunkie](https://gearjunkie.com/?p=504164)) [V]. Pub-anchored clubs ("post-run pint") are popular ([Bristol24/7](https://www.bristol247.com/lifestyle/health-and-fitness/bristol-europe-most-running-clubs-population/)) [V]. **Implication:** run clubs work because they satisfy all three of Adams' conditions, give side-by-side activity (good for young men), and are recurring by default. Switchboard's ritual templates should include activity-anchored formats, and should make "bring one friend" the growth path.

**F3. Supper clubs and dinners with strangers.** Timeleft matches strangers for weekly Wednesday dinners, with no profiles or messaging beforehand, only an invitation and an address. Its dinners launched in Lisbon in May 2023 (reportedly 20,000 subscribers and about 1,000 dinners within months), and in December 2025 it relaunched in Paris, which it calls the city "where it all began" ([Timeleft](https://timeleft.com/post/dinner-with-strangers-the-new-social-revolution-hits-paris/); [Timeleft](https://timeleft.com/post/back-to-basics-timeleft-launches-its-unique-dinners-in-paris/)) [V-corrected: the earlier "Paris, 2020" founding is unconfirmed after recheck]. Supper-club growth among Gen Z [R] (unconfirmed after recheck). Current Timeleft user totals [U]. **Implication:** strangers-at-dinner works when there is structure (a host, a fixed format, a small table). Switchboard's cascading invites (friends of friends) give a safer version: half-strangers, vouched for by one hop.

## G. Ethical design

**G1. Time Well Spent and the Center for Humane Technology.** Tristan Harris's "Time Well Spent" movement grew out of his viral internal Google presentation and became the Center for Humane Technology, founded in 2018 by Harris, Aza Raskin and Randima Fernando ([CHT](https://humanetech.com/about-us/); [CNBC, 2018](https://www.cnbc.com/2018/02/04/early-facebook-and-google-employees-form-coalition-to-fight-what-they-built.html)) [V]. It argues that engagement-maximizing design (infinite scroll, variable rewards, social-approval metrics) works against what users want [R]. Facebook's January 2018 change to rank by "meaningful social interactions" (MSI) was found in internal research to reward outrage, because heavily weighted reshares amplified angry content, and Zuckerberg resisted some fixes ("Facebook Tried to Make Its Platform a Healthier Place. It Got Angrier Instead.", Keach Hagey and Jeff Horwitz, WSJ, September 15, 2021, part of the Facebook Files series; [Election Law Blog excerpt](https://electionlawblog.org/2021/facebook-tried-to-make-its-platform-a-healthier-place-it-got-angrier-instead/)) [V]. **Implication:** even a well-meant engagement metric (MSI) gets gamed once it is optimized. Switchboard's metric must be measured **outside the app** (did people meet?), not by in-app activity.

**G2. Dark patterns.** The FTC staff report "Bringing Dark Patterns to Light" (September 2022) groups manipulative design into four kinds: designs that induce false beliefs (fake countdowns, false scarcity), hide or delay material information (such as fees), lead to unauthorized charges (including obstructed cancellation), and obscure or subvert privacy choices (data-grabbing defaults), with an appendix compiling many more ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/reports/bringing-dark-patterns-light); [Hunton](https://hunton.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/ftc-releases-report-on-dark-patterns); [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/blogs/2022/09/the-ftc-brings-more-light-to-dark-patterns-in-new)) [V for date and four-part structure; the last two headings are from memory, and one law-firm summary counts eight categories]. In social apps, the classic case is contact-book "friend finder" invite spam. In *Perkins v. LinkedIn*, a court found members had consented to importing contacts and the first "Add Connections" invitation but not to the two automatic reminder emails; LinkedIn agreed to a $13M settlement (notice October 2015, final approval February 2016), without admitting wrongdoing, and added controls to stop reminders ([Lieff Cabraser](https://www.lieffcabraser.com/?p=3837); [Techlicious](https://www.techlicious.com/blog/linkedin-to-pay-13-million-settlement-for-add-connections-service-spam/)) [V-corrected: final approval was 2016]. Harry Brignull's taxonomy is at [deceptive.design](https://www.deceptive.design/) [R]. Lapse's five-text gate and Gas's paid God Mode (Part 1) are recent examples. **Implication:** never upload address books silently, never send invites on someone's behalf that they did not compose or approve one by one, never sell hints that break anonymity, and never use streaks, guilt badges or "X is waiting for you" fake urgency.

**G3. The Surgeon General's "reform digital environments" pillar** (A1) asks for technology that supports healthy connection [V for the pillar's existence; its specific asks unconfirmed after recheck]. **Implication:** Switchboard can publish a short "what we don't optimize" statement as a trust signal (see the metrics section).

# (a) Twelve evidence-backed design principles for Switchboard

1. **The smallest audience that makes the plan happen.** Default to one-at-a-time cascades and small groups. *Evidence:* Close Friends increases sharing (Mosseri), Locket's cap, Dunbar's inner layers.
2. **Reveal desire only when it is mutual.** Mutual-interest matching, post-plan "again?" prompts and aggregate "people are up for something" signals with a minimum group size. *Evidence:* liking gap (Boothby 2018), pluralistic ignorance (Prentice and Miller 1993; Jordan 2011).
3. **Make asking feel as cheap as it really is.** Tiny first touches, vulnerable templates, and copy that states the research ("people appreciate this more than you'd guess"). *Evidence:* Liu 2022, Flynn and Bohns 2008, beautiful mess (Bruk 2018).
4. **Make "no" cheap and private, so "yes" means yes.** Anonymous polls and private declines with warm defaults. *Evidence:* awkward-yes compliance (Flynn and Bohns), decline costs overestimated (Givi and Kirk, JPSP, online 2023).
5. **Silence never hangs.** Non-responses resolve to a neutral state on a deadline, with no "seen" receipts and no visible absences. *Evidence:* rejection sensitivity (Downey and Feldman 1996), BeReal's late stamp.
6. **Failure is private, success is shared.** A collapsed plan is told only to the host, kindly. A plan that happened is celebrated with the people who were there. *Evidence:* spotlight effect (Gilovich 2000).
7. **Repetition beats novelty.** Standing rituals at consistent places, with low-participation attendance welcome. *Evidence:* Adams' three conditions, mere exposure (Zajonc 1968; Moreland and Beach 1992), ritual review (Hobson 2018).
8. **Count hours, not posts, and keep the count private.** "Time together this season" is a personal compass, never a public score or streak. *Evidence:* Hall 2018 (about 50, 90 and 200 hours).
9. **One owner, one next step, 2 to 3 options.** Turn "we should hang out" into a concrete proposal and close polls on a deadline. *Evidence:* diffusion of responsibility (Darley and Latané [R]), choice-overload caveats (Scheibehenne 2010).
10. **Warm ties before they decay, layer-aware.** Reconnection radar paced to Dunbar layers, with low-pressure async signals for the outer layers. *Evidence:* Roberts and Dunbar 2011, Liu 2022 (unexpected contact is valued most), Marco Polo's durability.
11. **Grow by real plans, not by toll gates.** Invites are a side effect of a real plan with a real person. Density goes one circle or campus at a time. No contact-book spam and no activation gates. *Evidence:* Lapse's spike and 70% fall, tbh/Gas/Fizz density, FTC dark patterns (2022), Perkins v. LinkedIn.
12. **Step out during the plan, return with the memory.** Quiet mode during the plan window, and memory capsules that "develop" the morning after, for attendees only. *Evidence:* phones reduce enjoyment (Dwyer 2018), Dispo and Lapse delayed reveal, Retro's weekly recap.

Cross-cutting rule from Part 1: **anonymity always protects the vulnerable party and is never for sale** (Gas's God Mode is the anti-pattern), and **no anonymous free text about a named person** (Yik Yak, Fizz).

# (b) Metrics

## Optimise (north star and supporting)

| Metric | Why | Measured how |
|---|---|---|
| **Plans that happened** per active user per month (north star) | Directly measures IRL connection, the outcome the Surgeon General and WHO care about | Plan reached its time with 2+ confirmed attendees, confirmed afterward by at least one attendee (a one-tap "it happened") |
| **People seen IRL** (distinct people per user per quarter) | Breadth across Dunbar layers | Unique co-attendees at plans that happened |
| **Hours together** (estimated, private) | Hall's hours-to-friendship | Plan duration times attendance, shown only to the user |
| **Repeat pairs** (share of pairs who meet again within 60 days) | Adams' repetition, and the strongest predictor of durable ties | Pairs in 2+ plans that happened |
| **Mutual "again" rate** | Closes the liking gap; measures quality | Share of post-plan prompts where both said yes |
| **Ask-to-happen conversion** | Measures whether friction is falling | Proposals that become plans that happened |
| **Ritual survival** (rituals alive after 3 and 6 months) | Durability without nagging | Rituals with at least one session that happened per cycle |
| **Reconnections** (dormant ties, 90+ days, that turned into a plan) | Reconnection radar's real job | Radar nudge, then a plan that happened within 30 days |
| **Self-reported connection** (quarterly, optional, 1 item) | Guards against gaming the IRL metrics | "In the last month, did you feel closer to people?" |
| **Time to plan** (median minutes from proposal to confirmed time) | Less coordination overhead is better | Lower is better |

## Refuse to optimise (track only as guardrails, or not at all)

- **Time in app, session length, DAU/MAU stickiness.** For a plans app, more time in the app often means coordination is failing. Track it only as a cost to drive **down**.
- **Notifications opened, push click-through.** Optimizing these produces BeReal-style alarms and guilt. Cap notifications per week instead.
- **Streaks, reliability or flake scores, public "late" badges.** Shame mechanics that turn rituals into obligations.
- **Invites sent, contacts uploaded, K-factor** as a goal. These reward Lapse-style spam. Measure invites **accepted for plans that happened** instead.
- **Messages sent or chat volume.** A busy group chat is often the death spiral. Plans, not talk, are the product.
- **Feed impressions, likes and reaction counts on room photos.** These create a performance audience. Memories are for attendees.
- **Number of friends or connections.** Dunbar caps meaningful ties, and bigger is not better.
- **Revenue from de-anonymization or visibility** ("see who's interested" upsells). This is permanently off the table: it breaks the mutual-consent promise, and Gas shows the reputational cost.
- **"Loneliness" as an engagement trigger.** Never target or message people based on inferred loneliness.

**Guardrails worth tracking (minimize):** unanswered invites older than 72 hours, plans collapsed with nobody attending (as a share), user-reported "felt pressured", give-space activations followed by contact attempts (a safety signal), and weekly notifications per user.
